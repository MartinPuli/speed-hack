# Taste Labs — event plan

Research reviewed September 28, 2026. Planning period: October–November 2026.

## Decision

Start with a Brand API workshop and a small Prototype research salon in San Francisco. Request a practical activation package from AI Engineer Code; do not commit before the complete fee, admission and rights are known. Vercel Ship is the fallback, not an automatic additional sponsorship. Keep the builders’ dinner conditional. Defer Lisbon and Helsinki unless Taste has an explicit Europe objective and separate staffing.

These are original recommendations based on public research, not actual Taste event announcements. Owned dates and venues are proposed. No organizer has been contacted, no sponsorship agreed, and no venue booked.

## Company evidence and audience inference

Taste’s public website describes a research and infrastructure company working on subjective domains, starting with design, and identifies SF as its base. Its Brand API offers brand extraction, style search and adherence verification for AI workflows. A public General Intelligence customer case demonstrates an integration use case. The Prototype fellowship points to research in human–AI interaction and machine creativity. These are distinct reasons to bring developers, agent product leaders and research collaborators into different rooms.

Primary company sources: [Taste Labs](https://tastelabs.com/), [Brand API](https://tastelabs.com/api), [customer case](https://tastelabs.com/case-study/general-intelligence-company), [Prototype](https://tastelabs.com/blog/introducing-prototype).

Inference: prioritize hands-on API adoption over general founder networking. A workshop should reveal integration friction and create concrete follow-up tasks. A salon should collect evaluation questions and research relationships. Sponsorship should buy practical access to builders, not only logo visibility. Neither conversion rates nor event ROI are known.

## Evaluation method

Compare audience fit, a practical activation format, relevance to a published product/research objective, lead time, location, cash, staff hours and conflicts. Recommendations are judgments, not numerical probabilities. Measurement targets below are planning goals, not forecasts.

Owned proposals use targeted applications/RSVPs and a small room. Partnerships require a full quote, concrete deliverables, clear admission terms, opt-in follow-up and appropriate recording rights. Do not treat a published sponsor logo as evidence of a contract’s economics.

## $15,000 cash scenario

This budget is an editable demo assumption, not Taste’s actual budget. Workshop high estimate $2,668 + salon $2,128 + Code operating allowance $2,070 + proposed sponsor fee ceiling $6,000 = **$12,866**. That leaves $2,134. Require Code passes, admission-related costs and package fees to fit the ceiling or revise the scenario. Do not silently count unknown passes as free.

Staff time: 18 + 12 + 48 = 78 hours. At an assumed $75/hour, internal time is $5,850. The combined modeled economic cost is $18,716 before any unresolved expense; internal labor is separate from cash. These estimates do not establish ROI or customer acquisition cost.

A smaller launch: workshop + salon = $2,806–4,796 cash and 30 staff hours. Under $3,000, start with the workshop alone and remove optional production before reducing participant experience. Keep 15% contingency. Quotes and attendance determine feasibility.

A partnerships objective could replace the salon with the dinner, but the high dinner estimate is $5,382 and should require eight qualified RSVPs before booking. Additional budget alone does not justify paying for more broad events.
